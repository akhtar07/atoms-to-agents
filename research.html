Machine-learned potentials · ongoing

Fine-tuning a multi-head MACE foundation model for high-entropy alloys

Foundation potentials promise “any element, any structure”. For the shear modulus of d-block high-entropy alloys, which matters for strength, how good are they really, and how few DFT calculations does it take to fix them?

  • Benchmark. Seven foundation models, zero-shot, on 304 structures across 19 training systems, with CoCrFeMnNi and VNbMoTaW held out.
  • DFT anchors. 1,870 VASP calculations (about 1.95M core-hours on PARAM Rudra).
  • Committee active learning. 10 fine-tuned members on 10 A100 GPUs (~1,250 GPU-h); their disagreement picks the next DFT runs. The share of uncertain snapshots fell from 54% to 36%, then to 21% in round 4.
  • Physics. Unstable stacking-fault energies 0.67–0.71 J/m² (V, Nb, Ta) and 1.47–1.80 J/m² (Mo, W); Rice ratio 6.7–8.1 for fcc and 1.8–3.6 for bcc. CoCrFeMnNi at 300 K: bulk modulus 143.6 GPa vs 143 GPa measured.
Still open. C44 comes out too low for V, Nb, Ta and W, and V is predicted unstable. CoCrFeMnNi’s shear modulus is 12% low (70 vs 80–81 GPa), and round 4 made that worse (−28%). Round 5 is next.

Undergraduate project (UGP), IIT Kanpur, which I designed and supervise day to day · manuscript draft.

Grain-boundary sliding by shear of oblate spheroidal units

Superplastic flow is explained by Padmanabhan and Schlipf’s model: sliding happens when small, oblate units of boundary atoms (about 5a0 wide, 2.5a0 thick) shear cooperatively. The model has stood for decades without anyone seeing those units. We went looking for them.

  • LAMMPS EAM bicrystals of Cu, Al, Fe and Mg sheared at 0.55 Tm.
  • Non-affine displacement (D²min) picks out the atoms that rearrange; a gyration-ellipsoid fit gives each cluster’s shape.
  • The clusters are oblate: thickness 2.4–2.8 a0, width 3.6–4.9 a0, oblateness 0.51–0.71.
  • For Cu the activation energy estimated from the simulations, 70 (59–81) kJ/mol, brackets the model’s 65.2 kJ/mol.

With Albert Linda (MPI für Nachhaltige Materialien, Düsseldorf), Somnath Bhowmick and K. A. Padmanabhan (corresponding) · manuscript prepared for Computational Materials Science.

Magnetism · data audit · draft

Most of the magnetic labels didn’t survive DFT

Materials databases often assign magnetic order from high-throughput DFT that explores only a few orderings. I set out to screen for Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya (DMI) candidates and ended up auditing the labels.

  • 64,635 records from 7 sources; a LightGBM classifier reaches 67.4% accuracy (balanced 68.0%; composition only 56.7%).
  • A physics funnel (ordering, broken inversion symmetry, a heavy spin–orbit element, stability) leaves 1,195 DMI candidates; the top 26 were re-checked with DFT+U against competing magnetic orders.
  • 21 of 26 database labels were overturned: a competing order was lower in energy by 0.19–24.68 meV/atom (largest for Ba3Fe2WO9). 3 were confirmed FM, 2 degenerate.
  • Case studies: Li4Fe2TeWO12 (J = 2.78 meV, D/J = 3.5%, Tc = 101.2 ± 1.3 K) and Tb6FeBi2 (Tc = 139 ± 3 K).
What this is not. It does not claim new skyrmion hosts or new materials. The 26 come from the top of one shortlist, not a random sample, so the rate applies to that family. It is a measured warning about the labels many ML screens are trained on. A fine-tuned MACE (51.1 meV/atom, 133.4 meV/Å) was not good enough to replace DFT here.

With Souradeep Bakshi, Rakesh Maurya and Somnath Bhowmick · code on GitHub

2D materials · draft

Electron doping weakens metal–ligand bonds, but bonds don’t predict phonons

The proposal was simple: add electrons to a 2D carbide or dichalcogenide, read the bond change with COHP, and predict which phonons soften. The first half held up perfectly. The second half didn’t, and the reason is the interesting part.

  • 22 monolayers (MXenes, carbides and TMDs), 46 bond types, 258 phonon modes, 154 doped states at ±0.2 e per formula unit.
  • Metal–ligand bonds weaken under electron doping in every one of 66 cases; only 3 of 13 metal–metal bonds follow.
  • An exact decomposition splits each frequency shift into an electronic and a structural channel. In TMDs the electronic channel dominates (91.7%); in Ti3C2Tx, the structural one (61.7%).
Negative results, reported. Bond changes predict the sign of a phonon shift only 48.8% of the time (324 cases), so the original hypothesis is refuted. A superconductivity screen was also negative.

With Vardhman Dwivedi and Somnath Bhowmick · draft.

COMPASS: which descriptors, and which DFT, actually matter

A hand-verified, cross-database resource for binary-alloy phase behaviour: 970 systems over 69 elements, cross-linked to 127,487 DFT entries. Thirteen bond-based orbital descriptors get close to a 132-feature black box while staying interpretable. It began as thesis chapter 3 and now stands on its own.
