PhD candidate, Materials Science and Engineering, IIT Kanpur

Md Faiz Akhtar

I study why titanium alloys are as strong (or as brittle) as they are, starting from the chemical bond. I use density functional theory and bonding analysis (ICOHP) to explain how small amounts of solute strengthen titanium and which slip systems operate, then carry those numbers through molecular dynamics and crystal plasticity (DAMASK, VPSC) and compare them with tensile tests at low temperature.

Over the last year I have also been building software agents that run this chain of calculations themselves and keep a record of every decision, so that a person can check the result.

Advised by Prof. Somnath Bhowmick and Prof. Nilesh P. Gurao. Prime Minister’s Research Fellow (2022–25). Thesis submission October 2026.

The PhD thesis

Quantifying the chemical bond

From Electronic Structure to Autonomous Multiscale Simulations of alpha Titanium Alloys. One premise runs through it: the mechanical behaviour of a metal is largely set by its chemical bonds. The integrated crystal-orbital Hamilton population (ICOHP) measures how strong a bond is. Resolved along crystal directions, it also shows which way the bond points.
