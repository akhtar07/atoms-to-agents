§ 01 The thesis, one scale at a time

Quantifying the chemical bond, then following it up the scales

From Electronic Structure to Autonomous Multiscale Simulations of alpha Titanium Alloys. One premise: the mechanical behaviour of a metal is largely set by its chemical bonds. Scroll, and each stop zooms out one scale.

01 · Electronic structurepublished

How strong is a bond, and does it predict strength?

A few hundred ppm of C, N or O transforms titanium; hydrogen does the opposite. The Ti–C bond is more than three times stronger than Ti–Ti (|ICOHP| 2.587 vs 0.757 eV). A parameter-free descriptor, Φeff, built from these bonds tracks the measured rise in yield strength.

Does it hold up in a real metal, at 143 K?

DFT fixes the slip-resistance ratios at 1 : 1.69 : 3.38; one prismatic value per temperature is calibrated. DAMASK and VPSC then reproduce cryogenic tension tests of CP-Ti from 298 to 143 K, where yield rises from 357 to 565 MPa.

One bond descriptor predicts how much an interstitial strengthens titanium

C, N and O raise the ideal shear strength of α-Ti by 3.5 to 17.6 %, while H softens it. Φeff, built from ICOHP bond strengths, follows the measured yield-strength ratios with no empirical fitting.

M. F. Akhtar, N. P. Gurao, S. Bhowmick, Journal of Applied Physics 138, 175111 (2025). Fig. 8.

HCP metals whose primary slip system R* gets right

Basal or prismatic? One number decides, including Be and Re

R* combines the c/a ratio with the electronic term J0/Ecoh. Below 1 a metal slips on the basal plane, above 1 on the prismatic plane, for every one of the thirteen HCP metals studied.

M. F. Akhtar, N. P. Gurao, S. Bhowmick, Materials Letters 400, 139019 (2025). Fig. 2.

Bonding plus short-range order predicts the strength of real alloys

Al, V and Mo prefer second-neighbour sites, so a random solid solution is the wrong reference; Mo shows the strongest ordering (about 229 meV). With it included, an ICOHP model predicts measured tensile strengths within a few per cent.

M. F. Akhtar, N. P. Gurao, S. Bhowmick, Materials Chemistry and Physics 368, 133019 (2026). Fig. 8.

Grain boundaries · LAMMPS

A sliding theory, caught in the act

Prof. K. A. Padmanabhan’s model says grain boundaries slide by shearing small oblate units. Finite-temperature MD in four metals finds them.

  • 6,029 sliding events in Al, Cu, Fe and Mg
  • each unit: a flattened disc about 1 nm wide
  • Cu activation energy 70 (59–81) kJ/mol vs 65.2 predicted

Cryogenic CP-Ti · EBSD + DAMASK

Colder titanium got stronger and more ductile. Why?

Tension tests from 298 to 143 K, EBSD before and after, and crystal plasticity whose slip ratios come from DFT, not fitting.

  • yield 357 → 565 MPa on cooling to 143 K
  • elongation rises from 28 to 36 %
  • twin fraction at 143 K predicted within 0.6 points, uncalibrated
